# Device performance data sources

_Last reviewed: 2026-09-20_

This comparison evaluates free sources for **observed clinical diagnostic performance** of ophthalmic devices and optical techniques: sensitivity, specificity, AUROC, repeatability, agreement, image-quality failure, examination time and related outcomes. It does not treat manufacturer specification sheets as independent performance evidence.

Scores are relative to this portfolio project, from **1 (weak)** to **5 (excellent)**. “$0 access” refers to source access, not compute, storage or manual validation effort.

| Data source | Database content | Project pertinence | AE learning value | DE learning value | $0 access mode and API documentation | Ease of access | Scope and granularity | Reliability / official status | Dependency on other sources | Principal limitation |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| **[Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/)** | Biomedical publications, preprints, abstracts, open-access full text, authors, affiliations, grants, references, citations, data links and text-mined concepts. | **5/5.** The richest of these sources for reported ophthalmic device performance, including OCT, fundus imaging, angiography, tonometry, optical biometry and AI-assisted diagnosis. | **5/5.** Supports an evidence mart at study × device × task × cohort × metric grain, plus study-quality segmentation, comparator models and confidence intervals. | **4.5/5.** REST ingestion, cursor pagination, XML parsing, full-text/table extraction, NLP, provenance and device-name entity resolution. | Free REST API and bulk/open-access services. Full text is available only where licensing permits. | **4/5 source authority; variable evidence quality.** Europe PMC is an official EMBL-EBI service, but it indexes studies rather than endorsing their methods or findings. | Depends on open-access availability for automated metric extraction. A curated device alias table and controlled metric/disease/reference-standard vocabularies are essential. Crossref or publisher pages are optional enrichments, not required for the MVP. | Heterogeneous designs make naive device rankings scientifically dishonest; many relevant values remain embedded in tables or prose. |
| **[ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/)** | Registered studies, sponsors, interventions, conditions, locations, eligibility, outcome measures, enrolment, status, submitted results, adverse events and linked publications. | **4/5.** Strong for study design and registered outcomes; useful performance results exist, but yield is much lower than the publication literature. | **4.5/5.** Rich trial, sponsor, site, intervention, outcome, arm and status-history models; excellent for distinguishing planned from reported evidence. | **5/5.** Modern REST/JSON API, arrays, pagination, bulk downloads, incremental snapshots, versioning and changing statuses. | Free public [API v2 documentation](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/about-api), [interactive API reference](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/api) and bulk JSON download. No API key is required. | **5/5.** Best-documented and easiest source here to automate; refreshed on business days. | Individual study/registration level with worldwide locations. Results are structured when submitted, but many records have no posted results. | **4/5.** Official NIH/NLM registry; content is submitted by sponsors and investigators. Registration does not validate a device or guarantee complete, unbiased results. | Stand-alone for a trial-evidence MVP. Europe PMC is needed for much denser performance extraction and published methods; device aliases are needed to normalize brand/model names. | Results sparsity and selective reporting mean that absence of a result cannot be interpreted as absence of evidence. |
| **[WHO ICTRP](https://www.who.int/clinical-trials-registry-platform)** | Trial-registration records aggregated from WHO-recognised primary registries worldwide: identifiers, sponsors, interventions, conditions, recruitment countries, study design, outcomes and result links where supplied. | **3.5/5.** Best global registry reach, but weaker than Europe PMC for numerical performance and less convenient than ClinicalTrials.gov for structured results. | **4.5/5.** Valuable harmonisation, deduplication, identifier crosswalk, source-registry lineage and global coverage models. | **4.5/5.** Multi-registry normalization, duplicate trials, changing source records, schema variation and incremental consolidation create serious engineering work. | Public search and downloadable search results; recurring new/updated record files and web-service access may require a request to WHO. See the [ICTRP data providers and access information](https://www.who.int/clinical-trials-registry-platform/network/data-providers) and [WHO Trial Registration Data Set](https://www.who.int/tools/clinical-trials-registry-platform/network/who-data-set). | **3/5.** Search use is easy, but dependable bulk/incremental access is less frictionless and should be confirmed before architecture is fixed. | Individual trial-registration level aggregated across international primary registries; broader geographic reach than any single national registry. | **4/5.** Official WHO aggregation with registry admission standards, but underlying records remain sponsor/investigator submitted and can be duplicated or unevenly complete. | Intrinsically depends on contributing registries. Requires registry and identifier crosswalks; Europe PMC is still needed for dense performance results. | Cross-registry duplication, heterogeneous fields and limited structured numerical results raise effort while lowering analytical yield. |

## Performance-specific decision

| Question | Best source | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Where are the actual sensitivity, specificity, AUROC or repeatability results? | **Europe PMC** | Published articles contain the most performance evidence, albeit buried in heterogeneous text and tables. |
| Which studies were planned, where, by whom and with which outcomes? | **ClinicalTrials.gov** | Cleanest public API and strongest structured registry model. |
| How can the registry view become genuinely global? | **WHO ICTRP** | Aggregates multiple national and regional registries, at the cost of access and harmonisation complexity. |

## Recommended boundary

For the first release, use **Europe PMC alone**, one modality—preferably OCT—and one or two diagnostic tasks such as glaucoma detection and diabetic macular-oedema assessment. Add ClinicalTrials.gov only after the metric-extraction and validation pipeline is credible.

The target fact grain should be:

**study × device/model × diagnostic task × patient cohort × reference standard × performance metric × threshold**

At minimum, capture publication/trial identifiers, device manufacturer and model, software version, modality, disease, setting, study design, patient and image counts, comparator, reference standard, metric, value, confidence interval, threshold, extraction method and validation status.

Never publish a universal “best machine” leaderboard. Performance is conditional on population, reference standard, threshold, study design, device generation and software version. A number shorn of those conditions is not a metric; it is decoration.
