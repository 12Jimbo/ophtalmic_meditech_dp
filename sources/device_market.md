# Device market and procurement data sources

_Last reviewed: 2026-09-20_

This comparison evaluates free public-procurement sources for answering: **who bought ophthalmic diagnostic equipment, from whom, when, where, and for what awarded contract value?**

Scores are relative to this portfolio project, from **1 (weak)** to **5 (excellent)**. Public procurement is a partial view of the market: it generally excludes private-clinic purchases and may omit contracts below publication thresholds. An awarded contract value is not automatically a unit price; it can bundle equipment, software, installation, training, consumables and maintenance.

## Price interpretation

Model the main amount as **observed awarded contract value**, not market price. Derive a unit price only when quantity and scope are explicit.

| Price confidence | Rule |
|---|---|
| **High** | Equipment-only award, explicit model and quantity, no material service bundle. |
| **Medium** | Quantity is known, but installation, software, training or limited service is included. |
| **Low** | Framework agreement, mixed devices, maintenance/consumables bundle, maximum ceiling, unclear quantity or ambiguous scope. |
| **Not comparable** | Missing/zero value, currency ambiguity, cancelled/no-award notice, or value represents a multi-year/multi-lot ceiling that cannot be allocated. |

## Recommended implementation sequence

1. **TED only:** build the stable EU-wide lot-level fact model and ophthalmic classifier.
2. **Measure gaps:** quantify missing suppliers, values, quantities and below-threshold coverage by country.
3. **Add one national source:** choose the country where the measured gap and hiring value justify the extra pipeline. Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Netherlands have the strongest structured-access cases in this shortlist.
4. **Defer Denmark and Belgium:** use TED first; add their portals only after a short spike proves lawful, repeatable, incremental extraction.

The canonical analytical grain should be **awarded lot × supplier × buyer**, with notice revisions preserved separately. Never force a one-row-per-notice model: one notice can contain many lots, suppliers, currencies, awards and corrections.
